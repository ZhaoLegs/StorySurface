Improve layout structure and spacing so the page feels authored, spacious, and product-first.

## Mandatory Preparation

Invoke `product-story-design` first. If no design context exists, run `teach-product-story`.

## Diagnostic Lens

Look for:

- repetitive section patterns
- equal spacing everywhere
- centered-content monotony
- card grids replacing actual hierarchy
- product visuals that feel secondary to layout chrome
- sections that do not advance the narrative

## Spatial Principles

Strong product-story arrangement relies on:

- long vertical pacing
- generous section spacing
- compact grouping inside a section
- contrast between dense and airy moments
- asymmetry when it sharpens focus
- one dominant visual anchor per beat

The page should feel sequenced, not merely assembled.

## Improvements To Make

### Narrative Structure

- Reorder sections to support a reveal.
- Lead with the clearest promise.
- Let each subsequent section introduce one major idea.
- Remove blocks that feel informational but not persuasive.

### Hero Composition

- Give the hero more room than feels strictly necessary.
- Reduce competing elements.
- Keep copy and CTAs visually subordinate to the core product image when appropriate.

### Section Rhythm

- Alternate visual density.
- Mix wide image-led sections with tighter explanatory sections.
- Avoid cloning the same left-right structure repeatedly.

### Grouping

- Tighten relationships between label, title, and support copy.
- Separate unrelated content more decisively.
- Use dividers only when spacing alone is insufficient.

### Cards

- Remove unnecessary cards.
- Flatten nested containers.
- Use full-bleed or lightly framed sections when the story benefits from openness.

## Verification

Before finishing, check:

- Does the page feel paced like a launch story?
- Is there a memorable visual moment in the hero and at least one later section?
- Does spacing create luxury rather than emptiness?
- If the cards disappeared, would the structure still hold?

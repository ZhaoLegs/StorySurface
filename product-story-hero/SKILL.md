Build the opening hero section for a product-story page.

This skill is specifically for the first screen or first major section. Its job is to establish desire, clarity, and hierarchy immediately.

## Mandatory Preparation

Invoke `product-story-design` first. If design context is missing, run `teach-product-story`.

Read:

- `../product-story-design/references/base-language.md`
- `../product-story-design/references/narrative-archetypes.md`
- `../product-story-design/references/product-page-patterns.md`

## Purpose

The hero must do four jobs quickly:

1. identify the product
2. communicate the core promise
3. create an emotional first impression
4. establish the primary next action

If the hero tries to explain everything, it fails.

## Hero Anatomy

Typical ingredients:

- product name
- very short tagline or stacked promise
- one primary CTA, with optional secondary CTA
- one dominant product visual
- optional metadata such as availability, price-from, or film CTA when product type supports it

For familiar hardware categories, default to an ultra-reduced hero:

- the product visual should dominate the screen
- the text block should stay compact
- keep only title, short support copy, and CTA
- add metadata only when it improves buying clarity without crowding the composition

## Writing Rules

- Keep the headline extremely short.
- Favor cadence over explanation.
- Let one line carry one idea.
- Avoid paragraph-length hero copy.
- Use supporting copy only if the product needs immediate clarification.

Typical hero voice:

- calm
- declarative
- controlled
- premium

## Visual Rules

- The product should dominate the first impression.
- Remove nonessential UI around the hero.
- Give the composition more space than seems strictly necessary.
- Use scale, crop, and contrast to make the product feel iconic.
- Keep surface effects subtle.
- Let the image occupy far more area than the text.
- Prefer one huge object moment over multiple smaller supporting visuals.

## CTA Rules

Prefer a compact pair:

- `Learn more` and `Buy`
- `Explore` and `Watch the film`
- one clear primary action when the page should stay especially quiet

Do not overload the hero with links, badges, stats, or chips.

If the hero starts to feel like a feature summary, reduce it further.

## Archetype Adjustments

### Flagship Launch

- lean hardest on imagery
- use the fewest words
- make the promise feel elevated

### Value Launch

- allow slightly more practical clarification
- make value legible early

### Creator Device

- signal flexibility, portability, or workflow fit quickly

### Performance Wearable

- keep language compact and active
- tie the product to life context or readiness

### New-Platform Manifesto

- use more declarative, category-defining language
- establish the mental shift immediately

### Sensory Experience Product

- sell a feeling the user can almost imagine physically

### Co-Branded Variant

- keep the page tight
- let brand affinity and product identity share the frame

## Quality Check

Before finishing, verify:

- Could someone understand the product promise in 3 seconds?
- Is the hero memorable without being noisy?
- Are there too many words competing with the image?
- Is the CTA system minimal and clear?
- Does the mobile hero still feel authored and iconic?
- Does the text occupy less visual weight than the product image?

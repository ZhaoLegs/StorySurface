# Component Patterns

Use these patterns as building blocks. Adapt them to the product rather than copying any specific brand layout.

## 1. Hero Statement

Use when the product needs one unmistakable opening message.

Structure:

- eyebrow or compact label when needed
- short headline
- one sentence of support copy
- paired CTAs
- dominant product visual

Guidelines:

- Keep copy compact.
- Let the image or render hold most of the emotional weight.
- Avoid adding too many secondary links or badges.

## 2. Cinematic Feature Section

Use for one major capability worth slowing down for.

Structure:

- strong visual background or product close-up
- concise section title
- one proof statement
- optional small supporting details

Guidelines:

- This section should feel more immersive than informative.
- Motion can help reveal depth or focus.
- Limit the number of competing text blocks.

## 3. Alternating Feature Beats

Use when showing 2-4 capabilities without collapsing into a generic grid.

Structure:

- alternating image-left and image-right rhythm, or alternating light/dark sections
- one core benefit per section
- tight supporting copy

Guidelines:

- Each beat needs a distinct reason to exist.
- Vary scale, crop, or backdrop so sections feel authored rather than cloned.

## 3.5 Full-Width Stage Section

Use when the section needs to feel like a product moment rather than a content card.

Structure:

- full-width band or very wide stage
- short label or section marker
- large headline
- one dominant product image, render, or scene
- optional compact support copy below or beside the media

Guidelines:

- Default to this pattern for hero, design, display, and lifestyle-proof sections.
- Let the stage breathe; do not over-frame it.
- Use rounded cards inside the stage only when they add a local proof surface.
- Keep the inner content width aligned with the rest of the page.

## 3.6 Image With Caption Block

Use when the image already carries most of the proof and only needs a short takeaway beneath it.

Structure:

- large image or media card
- short lead sentence directly below
- optional muted supporting sentence

Guidelines:

- Keep the caption tight and editorial.
- The lead line should usually read like the buyer takeaway, not like a feature list.
- Best for lifestyle proof, portability proof, app examples, and size comparisons.
- Common in computing pages where the image sells the feeling and the caption closes the argument.
- If text appears inside the card itself, keep it quiet and body-sized rather than headline-sized.

## 4. Comparison Or Ecosystem Band

Use when the product lives inside a family of devices or services.

Structure:

- short framing headline
- compact cards, chips, or columns
- one obvious next action

Guidelines:

- Keep this quieter than the hero and feature moments.
- Do not let informational density overpower the page's premium feel.

## 5. Fine Print Zone

Use for legal text, technical disclaimers, and pricing notes.

Guidelines:

- Separate clearly from marketing content.
- Reduce contrast without harming readability.
- Keep spacing clean and unobtrusive.

## 6. Closing CTA

Use to give the page a confident finish.

Structure:

- a compact restatement of the value
- one primary action
- optional secondary action

Guidelines:

- Do not repeat the whole page.
- End with clarity, not another feature dump.

## 7. Highlights Rail

Use when the product needs a quick, high-level sweep of its strongest selling points before deeper sections.

Common behavior:

- title like `Get the highlights.`
- short selectable topics or chips
- one sentence or two of proof per highlight
- optional film link such as `Watch the film`

Guidelines:

- Keep each highlight terse and visual-first.
- This section should feel like a curated preview, not a spec sheet.
- Best for flagship phones, value phones, and lightweight creator-tablet pages.
- A highlights rail can use cards, but the rest of the page should not inherit that card logic by default.

## 8. Closer Look Gallery

Use when industrial design or color/material selection is a core selling point.

Common behavior:

- title like `Take a closer look.`
- large product viewer or layered render stack
- color or finish selection
- optional AR or spatial-view CTA

Guidelines:

- Let the object itself be the interface.
- Minimize surrounding copy.
- Best for premium hardware tiers and hero design sections.

## 9. Capability Comparison Table

Use when the user must choose between variants or accessory models.

Structure:

- rows of capabilities
- columns for product variants
- very short explanations
- explicit compatibility statements

Guidelines:

- Keep language factual and concise.
- This section is utility-heavy, so visually simplify it.
- Best for accessory families such as stylus product lines.

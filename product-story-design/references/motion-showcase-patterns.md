# Motion Showcase Patterns

Use this reference when designing motion for premium product-story pages, especially hardware launches.

These patterns are distilled from recent product pages and from how their interactive sections are structured. Where a point refers to animated behavior rather than visible static structure, treat it as a design inference rather than a source-quoted fact.

## Motion Jobs

Motion on these pages usually serves one of five jobs:

1. reveal the product
2. clarify depth or material
3. transition between narrative beats
4. help compare variants or colors
5. make invisible benefits feel tangible

If motion does not serve one of these jobs, cut it.

## Common Motion Types

### Hero Drift

Used for:

- earbuds
- laptops
- phones
- watches

Behavior:

- slow floating or drifting object
- slight parallax between foreground product and background wash
- subtle fade and scale on entry

Why it works:

- gives the object a sense of weightlessness and precision
- makes the first frame feel premium without becoming loud

### Scroll-Paced Reveal

Used for:

- entering major chapters
- transitioning from beauty to technical proof
- revealing layered hardware details

Behavior:

- copy locks in first
- image or product detail catches up as the scroll continues
- sections feel stitched together rather than abruptly stacked

Why it works:

- preserves control
- makes a long page feel authored

### Color Or Finish Swap

Used for:

- phone colors
- laptop colors
- desktop finishes

Behavior:

- instant but refined state switch
- backgrounds or reflections may adapt slightly
- product angle often stays consistent so the color change reads clearly

Why it works:

- keeps attention on finish differences
- avoids disorienting the user

### Detail Zoom

Used for:

- ports
- chip visuals
- speaker mesh
- camera systems
- material surfaces

Behavior:

- move from product whole to product part
- keep transitions slow and crisp
- pair with minimal copy

Why it works:

- makes craftsmanship feel considered
- lets one product attribute own the frame

### Capability Simulation

Used for:

- ANC and transparency
- intelligence workflows
- continuity features
- display and media quality

Behavior:

- show the software or environmental effect, not just the hardware
- animate the state change that matters
- let the viewer feel the benefit instead of reading an explanation only

Why it works:

- translates invisible value into something legible

## Motion Principles

### Keep It Slow Enough To Feel Expensive

Fast motion can feel cheap unless it is tied to an explicit interaction response.

### Keep It Sparse Enough To Stay Believable

One strong motion idea per section is usually enough.

### Let Interaction Beat Decoration

If the motion helps color selection, comparison, reveal, or state change, it earns its place.

If it is only decorative, it usually weakens the page.

### Reduced Motion Should Preserve Meaning

When reduced motion is enabled:

- keep state clarity
- keep reveal order
- remove drift, parallax, and long interpolations

## Motion Anti-Patterns

Avoid:

- bounce easing
- constant floating on many objects at once
- spinning products for no reason
- flashy shader backgrounds that overshadow the device
- fast hover gimmicks on premium pages
- scroll hijacking that traps the user

## Best Use By Product Type

### Computing Devices

- hero drift
- thinness reveal
- color swap
- chip or display detail zoom

### Audio Products

- comfort and fit simulation
- ANC/transparency state transitions
- sound-as-space visual metaphor

### Wearables

- object rotation with strap or finish changes
- activity-context transitions
- safety or health highlight reveals

### Spatial Or New-Platform Products

- interface model demonstrations
- layered spatial reveal
- worldview chapter transitions

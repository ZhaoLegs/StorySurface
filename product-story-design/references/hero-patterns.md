# Hero Patterns

Use this reference for the first screen of a premium product page.

The hero is not a summary of the whole page. It is a controlled opening statement.

## Shared Hero Logic

Across product families, strong launch-page heroes usually share:

- product name
- a very short promise
- sparse CTA system
- one dominant image
- minimal competing interface chrome

The hero should feel iconic within seconds.

For the strongest first-screen executions on familiar product pages:

- the product image takes most of the area
- the text block occupies a small portion of the composition
- the copy usually stops at title plus one short support line
- the CTA system stays minimal
- most first screens do not need a huge text stack; strong section headings later in the page often work better around `56px` than `64px`

This is extreme simplification on purpose. The hero should present the product, not explain the page.

## What The Hero Must Accomplish

The first screen should answer:

- what is this?
- why should I care?
- what should I do next?

It should not try to answer every feature question yet.

In many successful heroes, the first screen is effectively:

- product name
- one short promise
- one short clarifying line
- one button or quiet CTA pair
- one huge product visual

Avoid adding highlights, spec bullets, chips, badges, extra paragraphs, or utility rows unless the product type truly demands them.

## Common Hero Shapes

### 1. Name + Tagline + Product Object

Most common across device pages.

Use when:

- the object is visually strong
- the product category is already familiar
- the page should feel maximally reduced and iconic

### 2. Name + Stacked Promise + Product Object

Common on more cinematic launches.

Use when:

- the promise benefits from rhythm
- the page wants a stronger emotional cadence

### 3. Name + Category Promise + Immediate Buy Cue

Common on value launches and some accessory pages.

Use when:

- buyers need fast practical orientation
- the category is clear and shopping intent is strong

### 4. Name + Manifesto Line + World-Shift Image

Common on new-category pages such as Vision Pro style experiences.

Use when:

- the product introduces a new model of interaction
- the hero must establish a category shift, not just a product object

## Copy Density By Product Type

### Flagship

- minimal copy
- maximum confidence
- image does heavy lifting

### Value

- slightly more explicit
- benefit clarity comes early

### Creator

- practical aspiration
- product and workflow both hinted

### Wearable

- compact and active
- suggests life context quickly

### Sensory

- emotional and experiential
- implies how the product feels or sounds

### New-Platform

- category-defining
- more declarative than descriptive

## Hero Anti-Patterns

Avoid:

- long introductory paragraphs
- chips, stats, badges, and links piled into the hero
- decorative gradients competing with the product
- multiple equally loud images
- headline systems that feel like startup marketing templates
- large text blocks that compete with the product image
- feature bullets, spec tiles, or highlight rails inside the first screen

## Mobile Hero Rules

- preserve the same emotional promise
- re-author line breaks carefully
- crop the product intentionally
- simplify supporting copy
- keep CTA reachability and clarity high

# Product Story Design Methodology

This document is the unified design summary for the `product-story-*` skill system.

Its purpose is not to document one brand's website. Its purpose is to extract reusable design principles, storytelling structures, interaction patterns, and implementation heuristics that can be applied to other premium product projects.

It is designed to be portable:

- for new product pages
- for redesign work
- for landing pages
- for premium hardware or software storytelling
- for design-system prompt context

## 1. What This Method Is

This method is for building product pages that feel:

- product-led
- calm
- premium
- edited
- clear
- emotionally persuasive without becoming loud

The key idea is simple:

good product storytelling pages are not just styled nicely. They are structured as controlled sequences of attention.

That means the method covers two layers:

1. a stable design language
2. a variable page narrative

The stable design language stays mostly consistent across projects.
The narrative changes depending on what the product is and what the buyer needs to understand first.

## 2. The Stable Layer

The stable layer is the part that should transfer across projects most reliably.

### 2.1 Product First

The product is the center of gravity.

Implications:

- imagery or the core experience should dominate the section
- interface chrome should frame the product, not compete with it
- decorative devices should stay secondary
- the user should remember the product, not the layout gimmick

### 2.2 Typography Carries Hierarchy

Typography is not ornamental. It is one of the main structural tools.

Principles:

- short headlines
- clear hierarchy
- limited visible font families
- restrained weight usage
- large, controlled display type
- readable body text

If the page still feels premium after removing the imagery, the type system is doing its job.

### 2.3 Whitespace Signals Value

Whitespace is used to create:

- focus
- pace
- separation
- perceived quality

The best result is not emptiness. It is edited spaciousness.

### 2.4 Surfaces Stay Quiet

Surface design should feel dimensional without becoming noisy.

Use:

- soft gradients
- subtle tinting
- crisp edges
- minimal shadow
- restrained layering

Avoid:

- effect-heavy glass as a default
- decorative glow without purpose
- layered card stacks everywhere

### 2.5 Color Is Controlled

A premium product page usually benefits from:

- a neutral base
- one controlled accent family
- strong color moments coming from the product itself

Color should clarify emphasis, not create chaos.

### 2.6 CTA Systems Stay Sparse

Calls to action should feel predictable and low-friction.

Common patterns:

- `Learn more`
- `Buy`
- `Compare`
- `Watch the film`
- `View in your space`

Too many links weakens the page's authority.

### 2.7 Motion Must Earn Its Place

Motion should usually do one of five jobs:

1. reveal the product
2. clarify material or depth
3. transition between narrative beats
4. compare variants or states
5. make an invisible benefit legible

If it does not do one of these, it is probably decoration.

### 2.8 Interaction Should Feel Effortless

The best interactions on these pages are:

- shallow
- focused
- tactile
- obvious
- low-friction

Good examples:

- highlight switching
- finish selection
- product viewers
- comparison toggles

These interactions are there to help attention, not to simulate a whole application.

### 2.9 Mobile Is Re-Authored

The mobile version should preserve the same hierarchy and emotional promise, but often with:

- different image crops
- different line breaks
- lower interaction density
- simplified composition

Do not just shrink desktop.

## 3. The Variable Layer

The page structure must change based on the buyer's first important question.

This is the part many teams get wrong.

They copy a beautiful page skeleton without checking whether that skeleton matches the product's buying psychology.

### 3.1 Narrative Archetypes

This system currently uses these archetypes:

- flagship launch
- value launch
- creator device
- accessory decision page
- performance wearable
- new-platform manifesto
- sensory experience product
- co-branded variant
- everyday computing device
- workstation computing flagship

### 3.2 How To Choose An Archetype

Ask:

- what is the buyer's first real question?
- is the benefit visible, invisible, or worldview-shifting?
- does the page need to persuade, explain, compare, or inspire first?
- is this about daily ease or professional headroom?

### 3.3 Archetype Cheatsheet

`flagship launch`

- best when the buyer asks: why is this extraordinary?
- emphasizes desire, materials, beauty, and premium capability

`value launch`

- best when the buyer asks: why is this worth it?
- emphasizes practical benefits and confidence

`creator device`

- best when the buyer asks: how does this fit my workflow?
- balances aspiration and utility

`accessory decision page`

- best when the buyer asks: which one should I choose?
- emphasizes compatibility and comparison

`performance wearable`

- best when the buyer asks: how does this help me all day?
- organizes sections by life context

`new-platform manifesto`

- best when the buyer asks: what is this new thing, and why does it matter?
- teaches a new mental model before drilling into features

`sensory experience product`

- best when the buyer asks: what will this feel like?
- translates invisible benefits into visual and emotional proof

`co-branded variant`

- best when the buyer asks: why this edition specifically?
- uses a shorter page with stronger affinity cues

`everyday computing device`

- best when the buyer asks: how will this fit into my everyday work and life?
- blends object appeal with approachable workflow proof

`workstation computing flagship`

- best when the buyer asks: can this justify serious work?
- keeps evidence and performance proof closer to the top

## 4. Common Page Skeleton

Not every page uses every section, but this is the most reusable default sequence:

1. product-local nav
2. hero
3. highlights
4. closer-look or object-led section
5. 2-5 feature chapters
6. ecosystem or platform section
7. comparison or buying help
8. closing CTA
9. fine print

This skeleton works only if each block answers a real buyer question.

If a block exists only because it appears on another page, remove it.

## 5. Module Patterns

The method is easiest to reuse when treated as modules, not as one monolithic page recipe.

### 5.1 Hero

The hero must do four jobs:

1. identify the product
2. state the core promise
3. create the first emotional impression
4. establish the next action

Hero rules:

- keep headlines extremely short
- let the object or core visual do most of the work
- use one or two CTAs at most
- avoid pills, badges, metrics, and clutter in the opening frame

### 5.2 Highlights

The highlights section is a preview rail, not a spec sheet.

Its job is to:

- summarize the strongest selling points quickly
- help the user see the shape of the story ahead
- keep the page moving

Good highlight behavior:

- one topic at a time
- one visual anchor per topic
- one sentence of proof

### 5.3 Closer Look

This section slows the page down and lets users study the object itself.

Best use cases:

- color
- finishes
- material details
- silhouette
- industrial design

Rules:

- make the product the interface
- keep text sparse
- keep transitions tactile and quiet

### 5.4 Feature Chapters

Feature chapters should be organized by decision category, not by internal team structure.

Examples:

- design
- performance
- display
- sound
- comfort
- health
- continuity
- safety
- battery

The user should feel a page rhythm, not a product requirements list.

### 5.5 Comparison

Comparison sections should intentionally become quieter and more utilitarian than the rest of the page.

Their job is to convert desire into decision.

### 5.6 Closing CTA

The ending should restate the value cleanly and then get out of the way.

Do not end by repeating the whole page.

## 6. Motion Principles

### 6.1 Typical Motion Types

The most reusable motion patterns are:

- hero drift
- scroll-paced reveal
- color or finish swap
- detail zoom
- capability simulation

### 6.2 Motion Quality Rules

Motion should feel:

- slow enough to feel expensive
- sparse enough to stay believable
- tied to hierarchy or interaction
- removable without breaking meaning

### 6.3 Reduced Motion Rules

When reduced motion is enabled:

- preserve state clarity
- preserve reveal order
- remove drift and parallax
- keep transitions minimal and functional

### 6.4 Motion Anti-Patterns

Avoid:

- bounce easing
- constant floating everywhere
- spinning products for no reason
- flashy background effects that overshadow the product
- scroll hijacking

## 7. Interaction Principles

These pages are not applications. They are storytelling surfaces.

That changes what good interaction looks like.

### 7.1 Good Interactions

Good interactions:

- help compare
- help inspect
- help reveal
- help understand a state change

### 7.2 Bad Interactions

Bad interactions:

- add cognitive load
- bury information behind novelty
- feel like UI demos instead of buying aids
- turn the page into a toy

### 7.3 Interaction Quality Checklist

Ask:

- is the interaction self-explanatory?
- does it reduce words?
- does it increase desire or understanding?
- does it still make sense on mobile?
- does it still work with reduced motion?

## 8. Copy Principles

Good copy in this system is:

- short
- calm
- declarative
- exact

### 8.1 Headline Rules

- one idea per line
- avoid stacked buzzwords
- avoid explanatory hero paragraphs
- use rhythm, not hype

### 8.2 Support Copy Rules

- one or two short sentences
- explain value, not internal architecture first
- cut repeated claims aggressively

### 8.3 Tone Rules

The best tone usually feels:

- certain
- unhurried
- premium
- not performative

## 9. Visual Design Heuristics By Product Type

### 9.1 Audio Products

Main challenge:

audio is invisible

Use:

- close-up industrial detail
- fit and comfort storytelling
- sound-as-space visual metaphors
- ANC and transparency state changes

### 9.2 Wearables

Main challenge:

the product lives on the body and inside daily life

Use:

- life-context chapters
- personalization and finish cues
- health, safety, and activity framing

### 9.3 Spatial Or New-Platform Products

Main challenge:

the user must learn a new mental model

Use:

- declarative hero language
- worldview chapters
- interaction-model demonstrations

### 9.4 Computing Devices

Main challenge:

balancing object beauty and workflow proof

Use:

- thinness staging
- screen-content curation
- continuity and ecosystem proof
- different evidence density for everyday versus workstation products

## 10. What To Avoid

These are the most common failure modes when teams imitate premium product pages badly:

- generic card grids everywhere
- centered-everything composition
- purple-blue AI gradients by default
- verbose copy
- too many CTAs
- effect-heavy glass or glow
- decorative metrics with no buying value
- repeating the same section format the whole way down
- using nice-looking mockups instead of meaningful proof

The failure pattern is usually the same:

the page becomes a polished template instead of a product argument.

## 11. Transfer Method

This is the part that matters most if you want to apply the method to other projects.

### Step 1

Define the product, audience, and core promise.

### Step 2

Choose the buyer's first question.

### Step 3

Pick the narrative archetype that matches that question.

### Step 4

Choose the section sequence based on real decision categories.

### Step 5

Apply the stable visual layer:

- product-first hierarchy
- restrained typography
- edited whitespace
- quiet surfaces
- sparse CTA system
- purposeful motion

### Step 6

Add only the interactions that reduce words or improve inspection.

### Step 7

Run a final reduction pass:

- fewer words
- fewer decorations
- stronger product emphasis
- cleaner transitions

## 13. Final Standard

The final page should feel:

- intentional
- calm
- expensive
- readable
- product-led
- memorable in one or two moments, not noisy in every moment

If the page feels like a template with nicer spacing, the method has not been applied deeply enough.
